A Kubernetes pull request waits 4 days for review, then merges in under 2 hours

By Gurnek Khaira · Snapshot of 4 October 2026 · 1,027 merged and 1,270 open pull requests in kubernetes/kubernetes

For 1 in 10, the review wait reaches about 7 weeks.

Software teams make changes through “pull requests” (PRs), proposals that a colleague must review and approve before they go in. This report looks at Kubernetes, one of the world’s largest open-source projects, and finds that changes spend almost all their time waiting to be reviewed and approved; once approved, they merge within hours. It shows which teams’ review queues hold the most waiting, and every number links to the real changes behind it, so anyone can check it.

Key findings

  • Getting a reviewer’s lgtm (“looks good to me”) took a median , and about 49 days or longer for the slowest 1 in 10. Once both review labels were set, merging took a median 1.7 hours.
  • of the PRs waiting for review (231 of 368) belong to two teams, sig/api-machinery and sig/node. Kubernetes calls its teams SIGs, short for special interest groups.
  • open PRs, 21% of the backlog, have had no human response, for a median of 41 days.

The largest queues

Each open PR sits in one team’s queue for one stage. Ranking queues by PR-days of waiting puts the ones with many PRs waiting a long time first. The unit is a team and a stage, never a person, because a long queue reflects capacity and process rather than anyone’s effort.

The author’s move is as common as the reviewer’s

337 open PRs are waiting on their author, against 368 waiting for a reviewer’s lgtm. Of the author’s queue, 175 need a rebase and only 21 have requested changes; the other 141 are waiting on a process label or the contributor licence check, most often a release note.

Approvals are not the constraint. Only 54 PRs have lgtm and are waiting for an approver, and 1 is waiting to merge.

Method and limits

How it was measured

  • Timelines come straight from GitHub’s API for every PR merged in 90 days and every open PR.
  • Review and approval are the lgtm and approved labels Kubernetes’ merge bot sets. If a label is removed and added again, the last add counts, so rework shows up as review time.
  • Bots and the PR’s own author never count as a response.
  • Only medians and p90 are shown. Groups under 10 PRs show — and are never ranked.

What it can’t show

  • Labels are a proxy: a PR waiting on a reviewer who is away looks the same as one waiting because the change is hard.
  • This is one snapshot, not a trend, and it doesn’t measure reviewer capacity.
  • It names teams and stages, never people.

Checked against DevStats

Kubernetes’ own DevStats charts PR velocity over time. Recomputing its “time to approve and merge” definitions on this data, for PRs created in August and September 2026, gives the same picture: most of the time goes to waiting for lgtm, and merging after approval takes hours. The medians differ by 16 to 28%; the cross-check explains the likely causes.
